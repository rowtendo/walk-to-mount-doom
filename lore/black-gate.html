The Gate of the Black Land

At the northwestern entrance to Mordor, where two great mountain ranges drew together, stood the principal gateway into the realm of Sauron.

It was called the Morannon.

The Black Gate.

To the west rose the Ephel Dúath, the Mountains of Shadow. To the east stretched the Ered Lithui, the Ash Mountains. Between them lay the narrow opening of Cirith Gorgor, the Haunted Pass.

Any great army approaching Mordor from the north had to reckon with this place.

And across it Sauron raised a gate of iron.

Yet the history of the Morannon was not merely the history of Sauron's defenses.

For centuries, the land immediately before the Black Gate had been watched by his enemies.

Mordor After Sauron

At the end of the Second Age, the armies of the Last Alliance of Elves and Men entered Mordor.

After a long siege, Sauron was overthrown.

But the victors knew that Mordor remained dangerous.

The Men of Gondor therefore established a watch upon the Black Land. Fortresses were constructed to guard important approaches and prevent Sauron's servants from returning.

Near the Morannon stood the great towers later called the Towers of the Teeth: Carchost and Narchost.

They rose upon either side of the entrance into Mordor.

In those days, they were not fortresses of the Dark Lord.

They were Gondor's.

Their purpose was to watch Mordor.

For a time, the prison had guards.

The Watch Fails

The watch could not be maintained forever.

As the Third Age advanced, Gondor suffered wars, internal conflict, plague, and declining population.

Its strength diminished.

The garrisons guarding Mordor were reduced and eventually abandoned.

The fortresses remained empty.

And while Gondor's attention turned elsewhere, evil returned to the land it had once guarded.

The irony was bitter.

Strongholds built to prevent Sauron's return eventually fell into Sauron's hands.

The watchers had departed.

The prisoner came home.

Sauron Returns to Mordor

For centuries Sauron had concealed himself at Dol Guldur in southern Mirkwood.

Then, in Third Age 2941, the White Council moved against him.

Sauron withdrew—but not because he had been defeated.

His plans were already prepared.

In 2951, he declared himself openly in Mordor.

Barad-dûr rose again.

Mount Doom awakened.

The Nazgûl gathered to their master.

And the old defenses of Gondor became part of Sauron's own fortified frontier.

The Morannon was now the principal northern entrance to a land preparing for war.

The Teeth of Mordor

On either side of the Black Gate stood Carchost and Narchost.

Once Gondorian watchtowers, they were now occupied by Sauron's servants.

Together with the mountains and the fortifications of the Morannon, they transformed Cirith Gorgor into a formidable barrier.

The gate itself stretched across the pass.

Battlements and towers guarded it. Orcs watched the roads. Behind it lay the desolate northern regions of Mordor and the road leading deeper into the Dark Lord's realm.

An army could not simply march through.

Nor could two Hobbits.

Frodo and Sam

In March 3019, Frodo Baggins and Samwise Gamgee came within sight of the Morannon.

Their guide was Gollum.

They had crossed the Dead Marshes and crept through the desolate lands before Mordor, hoping to find a way inside.

Then they saw the Black Gate.

The sight ended any hope of entering Mordor openly.

The gate was heavily guarded. Companies of soldiers moved along the roads. Watchers occupied the towers.

Frodo nevertheless believed that he might have to attempt it.

He had been charged with carrying the One Ring to Mount Doom, and he knew of no other road.

Gollum stopped him.

There was another way, he said.

A secret path.

A stair above Minas Morgul.

A pass called Cirith Ungol.

Frodo agreed to follow him.

The Black Gate had turned them aside.

And Gollum led them toward Shelob.

The Captains of the West

While Frodo and Sam struggled through Mordor, the great war outside it reached its climax.

Sauron's armies attacked Minas Tirith.

They were defeated upon the Pelennor Fields, but the victory did not destroy Sauron's strength.

His armies remained vast.

His fortress remained secure.

And the Captains of the West faced an impossible strategic problem.

They could not defeat Mordor by force.

But they did not need to.

They needed Sauron to look somewhere else.

Gandalf and Aragorn understood that the Ring-bearer might still be alive inside Mordor. If so, Frodo's only hope was secrecy.

So they devised a plan that appeared almost suicidal.

They would march upon the Black Gate.

The Last March

A force of approximately seven thousand set out from Minas Tirith.

It was tiny compared with the armies Sauron could summon.

Among its leaders were Aragorn, Gandalf, Éomer, Imrahil, and other captains of Gondor and Rohan.

They marched north through Ithilien toward the Morannon.

The strategy depended upon Sauron's greatest weakness.

He could understand the desire for power.

He could understand conquest.

What he could not easily imagine was that anyone possessing the One Ring would seek to destroy it.

Sauron therefore interpreted Aragorn's actions according to his own fears.

He believed a new Ring-lord might be challenging him.

The army approaching his gate seemed to confirm it.

Sauron's attention fixed upon the West.

Exactly as Gandalf had hoped.

The Mouth of Sauron

Before the battle began, a rider emerged from the Black Gate.

He called himself the Mouth of Sauron.

He was a Black Númenórean who had entered Sauron's service and risen high in his favor.

He came not to negotiate in good faith, but to break the resolve of Sauron's enemies.

He displayed objects taken from a prisoner.

A mithril coat.

An Elven cloak.

A sword.

They belonged to Frodo and Sam.

The Captains of the West did not know that Frodo had escaped from the Tower of Cirith Ungol.

For a terrible moment, the objects seemed to suggest that the Ring-bearer's quest had failed.

The Mouth demanded surrender and offered humiliating terms.

Gandalf rejected them.

He took the captured belongings from the messenger.

The Mouth of Sauron fled back toward the gate.

Then Mordor opened.

The Battle of the Morannon

The armies of Sauron poured out.

The Captains of the West were surrounded.

Orcs, Trolls, Easterlings, and other servants of the Dark Lord closed around them.

The western army formed its defense upon two hills of slag and rubble before the Morannon.

There was no realistic hope of military victory.

That had never been the purpose.

Every soldier standing before the Black Gate was buying time for two Hobbits whom almost none of them could see and whose fate none of them knew.

Pippin fought among the Men of Gondor.

He struck down a Troll, which collapsed upon him.

As darkness closed around the army, the end seemed near.

Then the world changed.

The Ring Is Destroyed

Far behind the Black Gate, Frodo and Sam had reached Mount Doom.

Inside the Sammath Naur, the One Ring fell into the fire.

Sauron's power collapsed.

Barad-dûr fell.

The Nazgûl were destroyed.

And the armies gathered before the Morannon suddenly found themselves without the will that had driven them.

The foundations of the Black Gate failed.

The Towers of the Teeth were ruined.

The forces of Mordor broke.

Some fled.

Some surrendered.

Others were swallowed in the destruction.

The battle that the West could never have won by strength ended because its true victory had occurred far away.

The Gate Falls

The Morannon had stood as a symbol of Sauron's power.

It had barred the entrance to Mordor.

It had guarded armies large enough to threaten the kingdoms of the West.

It had seemed immovable.

Yet its fall revealed the weakness upon which Sauron's entire dominion had been built.

Everything depended upon him.

When the Ring was destroyed, the power holding his realm together vanished with it.

The Black Gate fell on the same day as its master.

The Iron Threshold

The history of the Morannon is a history of reversal.

Fortresses raised by Gondor to watch the servants of Sauron became fortresses from which Sauron watched Gondor.

A gate intended to make Mordor impenetrable ultimately helped convince the Dark Lord that his enemies intended to attack him openly.

And an army that marched there with no hope of victory achieved exactly what it needed to achieve.

It drew the Eye away.

At the beginning of the Third Age, Men had stood before Mordor to ensure that evil did not return.

At the end of the Age, Men stood there again.

This time they did not come to guard the prison.

They came to keep its master watching the gate while two Hobbits crossed the land behind him.

And for all Sauron's armies, towers, spies, and fortifications, that was the danger he never saw until it was too late.
