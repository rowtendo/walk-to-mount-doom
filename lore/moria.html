The Halls of Durin

Before it was called Moria, before Orcs haunted its passages and travelers feared its gates, the great realm beneath the Misty Mountains bore another name.

Khazad-dûm.

The Dwarves called it the Dwarrowdelf, and among all the mansions of their people, none became more renowned.

Its history began with Durin the Deathless, eldest of the Seven Fathers of the Dwarves. According to Dwarven tradition, Durin awoke in the elder days beneath Mount Gundabad and journeyed south along the Misty Mountains.

There he came upon a dark and still lake beneath the mountains.

Looking into its waters, Durin saw stars reflected about his head like a crown. He took this as a sign and made his dwelling nearby.

The lake became Kheled-zâram, the Mirrormere.

Above it rose the three great peaks later called Caradhras, Celebdil, and Fanuidhol.

Beneath them, Durin's people began to delve.

And Khazad-dûm was born.

The Kingdom Beneath the Mountain

Over long centuries, the Dwarves transformed the roots of the mountains.

They cut roads through living stone, raised pillars in enormous subterranean halls, bridged chasms, carved stairways, and drove mines ever deeper beneath the earth.

Khazad-dûm was not merely a mine.

It was a kingdom.

Its passages extended from the Dimrill Dale on the eastern side of the Misty Mountains to the lands of Eregion in the west. Great gates opened at either end, allowing Khazad-dûm to become a passage beneath mountains that were otherwise difficult and dangerous to cross.

Generations of Dwarves lived and died within its halls.

And beneath the mountains they discovered a substance more precious than gold.

Mithril

The greatest treasure of Khazad-dûm was mithril.

Light, beautiful, and extraordinarily strong, the metal was prized throughout Middle-earth. The Elves called it mithril, the Grey-gleam. The Dwarves possessed their own secret name for it.

By the later Ages, Khazad-dûm was the only known source of mithril in Middle-earth.

Its value became immense.

Weapons and armor could be fashioned from it. The metal could also be beaten and polished to remarkable fineness. The shirt of mail later given by Thorin Oakenshield to Bilbo Baggins, and afterward worn by Frodo, was made from mithril mined beneath these mountains.

But the wealth of Khazad-dûm would eventually draw the Dwarves farther and farther into the depths.

There, something ancient was waiting.

Friendship with Eregion

During the Second Age, a realm of Noldorin Elves arose immediately west of Khazad-dûm.

It was called Eregion, or Hollin.

Unlike the suspicion that often existed between Elves and Dwarves, the peoples of Eregion and Khazad-dûm formed an unusually close friendship.

The Elven-smiths of Eregion were led by Celebrimbor, grandson of Fëanor. Their craft delighted the Dwarves, and the skill of the Dwarves was admired in return.

Among the closest friendships was that between Celebrimbor and the Dwarf Narvi.

Together their work was commemorated at the West-gate of Moria.

Upon its doors appeared words written in Ithildin:

The Doors of Durin, Lord of Moria. Speak, friend, and enter.

Narvi made the doors.

Celebrimbor drew the signs upon them.

For a time, the western entrance of Khazad-dûm was not a gate of dread but a doorway between friends.

Sauron's War

The friendship of Eregion and Khazad-dûm became important when Sauron revealed himself.

Having deceived the Elven-smiths and secretly forged the One Ring, Sauron invaded Eriador in the seventeenth century of the Second Age.

In Second Age 1697, Eregion fell.

Celebrimbor was slain.

The Dwarves of Khazad-dûm fought against Sauron's forces and aided the enemies of the Dark Lord, but they could not save the Elven realm.

After Eregion's destruction, the Dwarves shut the doors of Khazad-dûm.

Sauron hated them for their resistance.

Yet he could not conquer their mountain realm.

For many centuries afterward, Khazad-dûm remained powerful beneath the peaks.

The Shadow Beneath the World

Unknown to the Dwarves, something had survived beneath the Misty Mountains from an age far older than their kingdom.

In the First Age, Balrogs had served Morgoth, the first Dark Lord. They were spirits of fire and shadow, corrupted in the beginning of the world and made into terrible servants of his power.

When Morgoth was defeated, at least one escaped destruction.

It fled into the depths beneath the Misty Mountains.

There it hid.

For thousands of years the Dwarves knew nothing of it.

Then, in their search for mithril, they delved too deeply.

Durin's Bane

In Third Age 1980, the miners of Khazad-dûm awakened the hidden Balrog.

King Durin VI was slain.

The creature became known as Durin's Bane.

The following year, Durin's son Náin I was also killed.

The Dwarves could not defeat the terror they had awakened. Khazad-dûm, greatest of their mansions, was abandoned.

Its people fled.

Many followed Thráin I northward, eventually helping establish the Kingdom under the Mountain at Erebor.

The halls of Durin were left empty.

But they did not remain empty for long.

Moria

Orcs entered the abandoned kingdom.

What had once been Khazad-dûm became a place of darkness and fear.

The Elves called it Moria, the Black Pit.

The magnificent roads beneath the mountains became Orc-haunted passages. Chambers once filled with Dwarven craft stood silent. The great bridge over the abyss remained, as did the endless stairs, mines, halls, and wells.

And somewhere far below, Durin's Bane endured.

For centuries the Dwarves dreamed of reclaiming their ancient home.

But Moria had become one of the most dangerous places in Middle-earth.

Azanulbizar

In Third Age 2790, the aged Dwarven king Thrór entered Moria.

He did not return.

The Orc-chieftain Azog killed him, mutilated his body, and sent a message of contempt to the Dwarves.

The insult began the War of the Dwarves and Orcs.

For years the Dwarves hunted Orcs throughout the Misty Mountains.

At last, in 2799, their armies reached the eastern gate of Moria.

There, in the valley the Elves called Nanduhirion and the Dwarves called Azanulbizar, one of the bloodiest battles in Dwarven history was fought.

The losses were terrible.

Náin, son of Grór, was killed by Azog.

But Azog was in turn slain by the young Dáin Ironfoot.

The Dwarves had won.

Yet they did not enter Moria.

Dáin had looked through its gate.

There he sensed the terror still dwelling within.

Durin's Bane remained.

Khazad-dûm could not yet be reclaimed.

Balin's Colony

Nearly two centuries later, hope returned.

In Third Age 2989, Balin, one of the companions of Thorin Oakenshield, left Erebor with a company of Dwarves and entered Moria.

For a time, the attempt appeared successful.

The colony occupied portions of the ancient kingdom. Balin took the title Lord of Moria, and messages initially reached Erebor telling of their progress.

Then the messages stopped.

In 2994, Balin went to look upon Kheled-zâram.

Orcs attacked him.

He was slain.

The colony was besieged. One by one its members fell as Orcs closed around them and a terrible creature lurked in the waters outside the West-gate.

The final survivors recorded their fate in a book.

Then silence returned to Moria.

For years, no one outside knew exactly what had happened.

The Fellowship Enters Moria

In the winter of Third Age 3019, the Fellowship of the Ring attempted to cross the Misty Mountains.

Snow and danger turned them back from the Pass of Caradhras.

One road remained.

Through Moria.

Gimli hoped that Balin and his people might still be found alive. Aragorn feared the road. Gandalf himself was reluctant.

Nevertheless, the Fellowship came to the West-gate.

The once-friendly doors were shut.

Their inscription contained the answer:

Speak, friend, and enter.

At last Gandalf understood. He spoke mellon, the Sindarin word for "friend."

The doors opened.

Behind them lay darkness.

The Tomb of Balin

The Fellowship crossed abandoned halls and deep passages until they came to the Chamber of Mazarbul.

There they found a tomb.

Upon it was written:

Balin son of Fundin, Lord of Moria.

The truth could no longer be avoided.

Balin's colony was dead.

Beside his tomb lay the damaged record later called the Book of Mazarbul. Its final pages told of the colony's destruction: Orc attacks, the death of Balin, the loss of the West-gate, drums in the deep, and enemies closing upon the final survivors.

Then the Fellowship heard drums themselves.

The Orcs had found them.

The Bridge of Khazad-dûm

The Fellowship fled eastward through the ancient kingdom.

Orcs pursued them.

Then something else appeared.

Fire gathered in the darkness.

Gimli understood only that it was beyond his knowledge. Legolas recognized a Balrog and was filled with terror.

Gandalf knew what stood before them.

At the narrow Bridge of Khazad-dûm, he turned to face it.

The others escaped across.

Gandalf broke the bridge beneath the Balrog's feet, and the creature fell into the abyss.

But its whip caught him.

Gandalf fell with it.

The Fellowship fled from Moria believing him dead.

Far beneath them, however, the battle continued.

From the Deepest Dungeon to the Highest Peak

Gandalf and the Balrog fell into waters in the uttermost depths beneath Moria.

There Gandalf pursued his enemy through forgotten passages and tunnels known to neither Dwarf nor Orc.

At last the Balrog fled upward.

They climbed the Endless Stair, rising from the deepest foundations of Khazad-dûm to the summit of Zirakzigil, the Silvertine.

There they fought again.

Their battle lasted until Gandalf cast down his enemy, breaking the mountainside as the Balrog fell.

Durin's Bane was destroyed.

But Gandalf's body could endure no more.

His task, however, was not finished.

He was sent back.

The Empty Halls

The death of Durin's Bane removed the ancient terror that had driven the Dwarves from Khazad-dûm.

But Moria did not immediately become theirs again.

Orcs still inhabited the mountains, and the great mansions remained largely empty through the War of the Ring.

The Dwarves had other battles to fight, including the defense of Erebor against Sauron's northern armies.

Yet their memory of Khazad-dûm never faded.

According to the traditions preserved concerning the later history of Durin's Folk, a king named Durin VII, called Durin the Last, eventually returned to Khazad-dûm in a later age.

The descendants of Durin reclaimed their ancient home.

The Dwarrowdelf

Moria is remembered chiefly as a place of darkness.

But darkness was only the final chapter of a far greater history.

Before the Orcs, there were Dwarves.

Before the drums in the deep, there were hammers and songs.

Before broken bridges and abandoned chambers, there were illuminated halls, craftsmen, merchants, kings, and roads passing beneath the mountains.

The Fellowship saw only the ruins.

Yet even those ruins revealed the scale of what had been lost.

Khazad-dûm had survived the wars of Sauron when kingdoms around it fell. Its people had befriended the Elves of Eregion and carved works that endured for thousands of years. Its wealth had become legendary.

In the end, its greatest treasure also led its miners toward their greatest terror.

For beneath the mountains, the Dwarves had built one of the wonders of Middle-earth.

And beneath that wonder, something older had been sleeping.
