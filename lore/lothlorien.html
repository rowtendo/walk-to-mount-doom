The Forest Beyond the Mountains

East of the Misty Mountains, between the silver waters of the Celebrant and the broad river Anduin, there stood in the Third Age a forest unlike any other in Middle-earth.

Men called it Lórien.

The Rohirrim, who feared its hidden people and the power of its Lady, called it Dwimordene.

Among the Elves it bore several names, including Lórinand and Lothlórien.

To those permitted beneath its boughs, however, it seemed almost untouched by time.

Golden leaves trembled upon silver branches. Clear waters descended from the mountains. Elves lived high among the trees upon platforms called flets, or telain. At the heart of the forest stood Caras Galadhon, the City of the Trees.

There dwelt Galadriel and Celeborn.

Yet Lothlórien was ancient before either became its ruler.

The Silvan Elves

The earliest inhabitants of Lórien were primarily Silvan Elves, descendants of those Teleri who had never completed the Great Journey into the West.

They were woodland people, less learned in the arts and histories of the Eldar than the High Elves who had returned from Aman, but deeply rooted in the forests and rivers of Middle-earth.

Their realm grew on both sides of the Anduin in its earlier history, though its heart eventually lay west of the Great River.

During the Second Age, the Silvan Elves of this region came under the rule of Amdír, an Elf of Sindarin origin according to one of Tolkien's principal accounts.

Their land was then commonly known as Lórinand.

Across the Misty Mountains lay another realm that would profoundly influence its future.

Eregion.

Galadriel and Celeborn

The precise movements of Galadriel and Celeborn during the Second Age vary among Tolkien's later accounts, and no single reconstruction resolves every detail.

What is clear is that they became closely associated with the lands around Lórien and ultimately made the Golden Wood their permanent home.

Galadriel was among the greatest of the Eldar remaining in Middle-earth.

Born in Valinor before the rising of the Sun, she was a daughter of Finarfin and had witnessed the rebellion of the Noldor, the long wars against Morgoth, and the ruin of Beleriand.

Celeborn was himself of ancient Elven lineage.

Together they had survived an Age in which kingdoms far mightier than Lórien had disappeared.

Their arrival brought to the woodland realm knowledge and memory reaching back to the Elder Days.

But another arrival transformed the forest itself.

The Golden Trees

In the Second Age, mallorn trees were introduced into Lórien.

These extraordinary trees had golden leaves that remained upon their branches through winter, falling only when new green leaves appeared in spring. Their bark was smooth and silver-grey.

They flourished in Lórien as nowhere else in Middle-earth.

In time, their golden canopy became so closely associated with the realm that it acquired names such as Laurelindórenan—the Valley of Singing Gold—and Lothlórien, the Dreamflower.

The greatest concentration of mallorns grew around Caras Galadhon, where the Galadhrim built their dwellings among the branches.

The forest was becoming the Golden Wood remembered at the end of the Third Age.

Sauron and the Second Age

Lórien could not remain entirely apart from the wars beyond its borders.

In the seventeenth century of the Second Age, Sauron made war upon the Elves of Eregion.

Eregion was destroyed.

Refugees fled in several directions, and the balance of power throughout the region changed. Khazad-dûm closed its western doors against Sauron, while Elrond established the refuge of Imladris farther north.

Lórien survived.

Later, when the Last Alliance of Elves and Men marched against Sauron, Amdír led warriors from Lórien to war.

In the Battle of Dagorlad, the Silvan Elves fought bravely but suffered grievous losses.

Amdír was slain.

His son Amroth became king.

Amroth

The memory of Amroth lingered long after his reign.

He lived in a high house among the trees and became associated with one of Lórien's most sorrowful tales.

Amroth loved Nimrodel, a Silvan Elf who dwelt beside the stream that later bore her name. She distrusted the changes brought by outsiders and grieved over the growing troubles of Middle-earth.

Then catastrophe struck the Dwarven realm across the mountains.

In Third Age 1980, the Dwarves of Khazad-dûm awakened Durin's Bane.

The following year Khazad-dûm was abandoned.

Orcs spread through the mountains, and fear came upon the neighboring lands.

Nimrodel fled Lórien.

Amroth followed her.

They intended to sail together into the West, but they were separated during their journey. Amroth reached the haven of Edhellond and waited for her.

She never came.

A storm drove his ship out into the bay. Believing Nimrodel might still be somewhere upon the shore, Amroth leapt into the sea and attempted to swim back.

He was never seen again.

The line of Lórien's kings ended.

The Lord and Lady

After Amroth's disappearance, Galadriel and Celeborn became the rulers and guardians of Lórien.

They did not take the titles of king and queen.

Instead, they became known as the Lord and Lady of the Galadhrim.

Their task was increasingly defensive.

Khazad-dûm had become Moria. Orcs infested the Misty Mountains. Dol Guldur cast a growing shadow from southern Mirkwood. The peoples of Eriador and Rhovanion declined, while Sauron's servants multiplied.

Yet Lórien endured.

And at the heart of its preservation was one of the Three Rings.

Nenya

Galadriel possessed Nenya, the Ring of Water.

It was one of the Three Rings of the Elves, forged by Celebrimbor without Sauron's direct touch.

Nenya was made of mithril and bore a white stone.

Through its power, Galadriel was able to preserve and protect Lórien against the decay and weariness of time.

The Ring did not freeze the realm literally outside time. Seasons still passed. Leaves fell. Years accumulated.

But within Lórien, the beauty and memory of the Elder Days were preserved to a degree found almost nowhere else in Middle-earth.

To outsiders, the effect could be bewildering.

A traveler might leave the forest uncertain how many days had passed beneath its trees.

The Golden Wood seemed less like a country untouched by time than a place where time was remembered differently.

The Shadow Across the River

Lórien's greatest enemy stood not far away.

Across the Anduin, in southern Mirkwood, rose the fortress of Dol Guldur.

For many centuries a mysterious power known as the Necromancer dwelt there.

Eventually the Wise discovered the truth.

The Necromancer was Sauron.

Thus the Golden Wood and the stronghold of the Dark Lord's servants stood almost opposite one another across the river.

Galadriel participated in the counsels of the White Council, alongside Elrond, Gandalf, Saruman, and others of the Wise.

In Third Age 2941, the Council moved against Dol Guldur.

Sauron withdrew.

But his retreat was deliberate.

He soon returned openly to Mordor, and Dol Guldur was occupied by powerful servants of the Enemy.

The threat to Lórien had merely entered its final stage.

The Fellowship

In January 3019, nine travelers emerged from the eastern gate of Moria.

Only eight remained together.

Gandalf had fallen while fighting the Balrog upon the Bridge of Khazad-dûm.

Grieving and exhausted, the Fellowship entered Lórien.

They were met by Elves led by Haldir, who guided them deeper into the forest.

For Gimli, a Dwarf entering the Golden Wood, the journey carried the weight of ancient distrust between their peoples.

For Legolas, it was an encounter with another Elven realm.

For Aragorn, it was a return to a place deeply connected with his past.

For Frodo, it became something more.

He was brought before Galadriel.

And Galadriel knew what he carried.

The Mirror of Galadriel

Galadriel tested the members of the Fellowship in ways that were subtle rather than openly spoken.

To Frodo and Sam she revealed the Mirror of Galadriel, a basin of water in which visions could appear—things that were, things that had been, and things that might yet come to pass.

Frodo then offered Galadriel the One Ring.

For a moment she confronted the temptation she had long feared.

With the Ring, she might attempt to overthrow Sauron and rule in his place.

She refused it.

In doing so, Galadriel passed a test bound to her long history in Middle-earth.

She accepted that her power there would diminish and that, at last, she could depart into the West.

The fate of Lórien was therefore tied directly to Frodo's quest.

If the One Ring survived, Sauron might conquer the Golden Wood.

If it were destroyed, Nenya would lose the power by which much of Lórien had been preserved.

Victory itself would bring an ending.

Gifts at the River

When the Fellowship departed, the Elves supplied them with boats, provisions, Elven-cloaks, and other gifts.

Galadriel gave each traveler something suited to the road ahead.

To Aragorn she gave the Elessar, the green Elfstone.

To Legolas, a bow.

To Gimli, after he asked only for a single strand of her hair, she gave three.

To Sam she gave a small box containing earth from her orchard and a silver mallorn seed.

To Frodo she gave a crystal phial containing light captured from her fountain, in which shone the light of Eärendil's star.

That light would later shine in the darkness of Shelob's lair.

The Fellowship then passed down the Anduin.

Behind them, Lórien prepared for war.

The War Comes to Lórien

During the War of the Ring, armies from Dol Guldur attacked Lórien three times.

The Galadhrim resisted them.

Tolkien records that the realm might have suffered grievously had it not been for the valor of the Elves and the power dwelling there.

Lórien did not fall.

After Sauron's destruction, the Elves crossed the Anduin and attacked Dol Guldur.

Celeborn led the forces of Lórien.

Galadriel threw down the fortress's walls and laid bare its pits.

The long shadow across the river was ended.

For a brief moment, Lórien had survived every enemy raised against it.

But the greater change had already begun.

The Fading of the Golden Wood

The One Ring was destroyed on 25 March 3019.

With its destruction, the power of the Three Rings faded.

Nenya could no longer preserve Lórien as it had before.

No army conquered the Golden Wood.

No Dark Lord burned Caras Galadhon.

Instead, its Age simply passed.

The Elves began to depart.

In Third Age 3021, Galadriel journeyed to the Grey Havens and sailed into the West with Elrond, Gandalf, Frodo, and Bilbo.

Celeborn remained in Lórien for a time before eventually leaving for Rivendell.

As the years of the Fourth Age passed, the Golden Wood grew increasingly empty.

The songs beneath the mallorns became fewer.

Arwen

There was, however, one final sorrow bound to Lórien.

After the death of King Elessar in Fourth Age 120, Arwen left Minas Tirith.

She traveled north alone.

The great Elven realms of her family had faded. Rivendell was diminished. Galadriel had departed across the Sea.

Arwen came at last to Lórien.

The land was silent.

She went to Cerin Amroth, the green hill where she and Aragorn had pledged themselves to one another long before.

There, in the winter of Fourth Age 121, Arwen lay down beneath the fading trees.

And there she died.

Her grave remained upon Cerin Amroth until the world had changed and the memory of her life had passed beyond the knowledge of later Men.

The Golden Wood

Lothlórien was never merely a forest protected by Elven magic.

It was a remnant.

Within its borders survived something of the beauty of an older Middle-earth: the memory of Beleriand, the wisdom of the Eldar, the songs of the Silvan Elves, and the light of a world slowly departing.

That was both its glory and its sadness.

Sauron could threaten it.

Armies could assail it.

But its true enemy was one that no sword could defeat.

Time.

For thousands of years, Galadriel preserved the Golden Wood while the world beyond its borders changed.

Then the Ring was destroyed, the Shadow passed, and the dominion of Men began.

The mallorns still stood for a while beside the Celebrant.

But the people who had sung beneath them were leaving.

And so Lothlórien achieved something rarer than victory.

It survived long enough to see the world for which it had fought become a world that no longer needed it.
