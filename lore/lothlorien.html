The Silvan Realm

Lothlórien was an Elven realm east of the Misty Mountains, lying chiefly between the river Celebrant, or Silverlode, and the Great River Anduin. By the end of the Third Age it was one of the most important surviving Elven refuges in Middle-earth, but its history began long before the rule of Galadriel and Celeborn. Its people were known as the Galadhrim, the Tree-people, and the heart of their realm lay in the woodland later famous for its golden mallorn trees.

The earliest inhabitants of the region were primarily Silvan Elves, descended from Telerin Elves who had not completed the Great Journey westward to Aman. Communities of these Elves developed in the forests and river valleys on both sides of the Anduin, including the lands that later became Lothlórien and the Woodland Realm. After the destruction of Beleriand at the end of the First Age, some Sindarin Elves also moved east and became leaders among these Silvan populations.

By the Second Age, an established Silvan realm existed in the region later known as Lórien. Tolkien used several names for the country over the course of its history, including Lórinand, Lórien, Laurelindórenan, and Lothlórien. The precise early political history varies among Tolkien's surviving accounts, but one important version names the Sindarin Elf Amdír, also called Malgalad in another text, as king of the Silvan Elves there.

Galadriel, Celeborn, and the Second Age

Galadriel and Celeborn also became closely associated with the lands around Lórinand during the Second Age. Tolkien repeatedly revised their history, particularly their movements between Eregion, Lórinand, Lindon, and other regions, so no single reconstruction of this period fits every surviving text. What can be stated securely is that Lórinand already existed as a Silvan realm independently of Galadriel and Celeborn, and they should not be described as its founders.

Galadriel herself was one of the oldest and most powerful of the Eldar remaining in Middle-earth. Born in Valinor before the rising of the Sun, she was a daughter of Finarfin and had lived through the exile of the Noldor, the wars against Morgoth, and the destruction of Beleriand. Celeborn was likewise of ancient Elven lineage, although Tolkien's accounts of his ancestry differ. Their eventual association with Lórien therefore brought the Silvan realm into closer contact with traditions and memories reaching back to the Elder Days.

The famous mallorn trees also became established in Lórien during the Second Age. Mallorn seeds had reached Middle-earth through Númenor, and Galadriel eventually planted them in Lórinand, where they flourished exceptionally well. Their silver-grey trunks and leaves that turned golden before falling in spring transformed the appearance of the forest and contributed to names such as Laurelindórenan, the Valley of Singing Gold. By the Third Age, these trees had become one of the defining characteristics of Lothlórien.

The War of the Elves and Sauron

West of the Misty Mountains lay Eregion, a Noldorin realm whose craftsmen maintained unusually close relations with the Dwarves of neighboring Khazad-dûm. Its greatest smiths were the Gwaith-i-Mírdain, led by Celebrimbor, and during the Second Age they were deceived by Sauron while he appeared in a fair form as Annatar. Their work eventually produced the Rings of Power, while Sauron secretly forged the One Ring in Mount Doom in an attempt to dominate the others.

When the Elves became aware of Sauron's betrayal, he went to war against them. His invasion of Eriador began in Second Age 1695, and in 1697 Eregion was destroyed and Celebrimbor killed. Elrond withdrew with survivors and established Rivendell, while the Dwarves of Khazad-dûm shut their western gates after fighting against Sauron's forces. Lórinand survived east of the mountains, but the destruction of Eregion permanently changed the political world surrounding it.

The war also affected Galadriel and Celeborn, though Tolkien's differing versions make their exact movements during these years uncertain. In some accounts Galadriel had already crossed through Khazad-dûm into Lórinand before Eregion fell, while Celeborn remained behind for a time because of his distrust of the Dwarves. The important historical point is that the Silvan realm survived Sauron's western campaigns and continued to exist after Eregion had disappeared.

The Last Alliance and the Death of Amdír

Near the end of the Second Age, Gil-galad and Elendil formed the Last Alliance of Elves and Men against Sauron. Amdír joined the alliance and led a force of Silvan Elves south from Lórinand. Another Silvan host came from the Woodland Realm under Oropher, father of Thranduil, so the Elves of the Anduin valleys participated directly in the final campaign against Sauron.

At the Battle of Dagorlad, the Silvan forces suffered severe losses. Tolkien describes them as courageous but less well armed and less accustomed to the organization of large armies than the forces commanded by Gil-galad. Amdír was killed after his warriors became separated from the main host in the marshes, while Oropher was also slain during the fighting. Amdír's son Amroth consequently inherited the kingship of Lórinand and ruled the realm into the Third Age.

Sauron was eventually overthrown after the long siege of Barad-dûr, and the One Ring was taken from him by Isildur. Lórinand thus entered the Third Age with a new king and with Sauron's power temporarily broken, although the Elven population of Middle-earth had been further diminished by the war.

The Reign of Amroth

Amroth ruled Lórien for many centuries during the Third Age. He was closely associated with the Silvan traditions of the realm and was remembered as dwelling among the trees rather than maintaining the kind of stone-built royal court found in the kingdoms of Men. Tolkien records comparatively few political events from the earlier centuries of his reign, suggesting that Lórien remained relatively stable while the wider fortunes of Elves and Men declined.

Amroth's story became closely connected with Nimrodel, a Silvan Elf who lived beside the stream that afterward bore her name. Nimrodel was strongly attached to the language and customs of the Silvan Elves and disliked some of the cultural changes brought by Sindarin and Noldorin influence. Amroth loved her, but the circumstances that eventually drove them from Lórien arose not within the forest itself but beneath the neighboring Misty Mountains.

For thousands of years, the Dwarven kingdom of Khazad-dûm had been one of the strongest powers in the region and an important route between the lands east and west of the mountains. Its existence helped shape the history of both Eregion and Lórien, but in the late Third Age the Dwarves' mining reached something that had remained hidden since the wars of the First Age.

The Fall of Khazad-dûm

In Third Age 1980, the Dwarves of Khazad-dûm awakened a Balrog of Morgoth beneath the mountains. The creature killed King Durin VI and became known to the Dwarves as Durin's Bane. The following year it killed Náin I, and the surviving Dwarves abandoned their ancient kingdom, leaving Khazad-dûm to become the dark and dangerous place later known as Moria.

The disaster had major consequences for Lórien. Orcs multiplied in the Misty Mountains, communication across the range became more dangerous, and the powerful Dwarven realm that had existed beside Lórien for much of its history suddenly disappeared. The resulting fear and unrest caused some of the Silvan Elves to leave their homes, among them Nimrodel.

Nimrodel fled south, and Amroth followed her. They intended to reunite and sail together from Middle-earth, but they became separated during their journey toward the Elven haven of Edhellond near the Bay of Belfalas. Amroth reached the coast and waited for her, but Nimrodel never arrived.

When a storm drove Amroth's ship away from the shore, he believed Nimrodel might still be somewhere on land and leapt into the sea in an attempt to return. He was never seen again, and Nimrodel's ultimate fate is likewise unknown. Amroth's disappearance ended the line of kings that had ruled Lórien since at least the later Second Age.

Galadriel and Celeborn Take Up the Rule

After Amroth's disappearance, Galadriel and Celeborn became the principal rulers of Lórien. They did not take the titles of king and queen, instead becoming known as the Lord and Lady of the Galadhrim. Their leadership came at a dangerous period, when Khazad-dûm had fallen, Orcs occupied the mountains, and Sauron's influence was again growing in the lands east of the Anduin.

Galadriel possessed Nenya, the Ring of Water, one of the Three Rings made by Celebrimbor without Sauron's direct participation. After Sauron lost the One Ring, she was able to use Nenya to help preserve Lórien against the decay and weariness of time. Tolkien does not describe this as literally stopping time, but the Ring helped maintain the realm in a condition that reminded visitors of the older Elven world that had disappeared elsewhere.

During the later Third Age, the principal settlement of the Galadhrim was Caras Galadhon, situated among enormous mallorn trees. Many of its dwellings were constructed upon platforms called telain, or flets, among the branches, while the city itself was protected by a wall and ditch. Lórien under Galadriel and Celeborn therefore combined its older Silvan traditions with the influence of rulers whose memories reached back to the First Age.

The Shadow of Dol Guldur

The greatest external threat to Lórien during the later Third Age developed across the Anduin in southern Greenwood. Around Third Age 1050, a shadow fell upon the forest, and a stronghold known as Dol Guldur became the dwelling of a mysterious power called the Necromancer. Greenwood gradually became known as Mirkwood as fear and evil creatures spread through the forest.

The Wise eventually discovered that the Necromancer was Sauron, who had survived his defeat at the end of the Second Age and was slowly rebuilding his strength. Dol Guldur's position placed one of Sauron's principal strongholds relatively close to Lórien, making the Golden Wood an important obstacle to his influence in the upper Anduin valley.

Galadriel became a leading member of the White Council, together with Elrond, Gandalf, Saruman, and others of the Wise. When the Council was formed in Third Age 2463, Galadriel favored Gandalf as its leader, but Gandalf refused the position and Saruman became its chief. Saruman later delayed action against Dol Guldur while secretly searching for the One Ring and studying the means by which he might obtain it himself.

The White Council and Sauron's Return

In Third Age 2941, the White Council finally moved against Dol Guldur. Sauron withdrew from the fortress, but his departure was not a true defeat; he had already prepared for his return to Mordor and soon declared himself there openly. Dol Guldur was later occupied again by his servants, leaving Lórien exposed to a renewed threat from across the Anduin.

During these centuries, Lórien became increasingly isolated from the peoples around it. Moria remained dangerous to the west, Dol Guldur threatened from the east, and the Elven populations of Middle-earth continued to diminish as many departed across the Sea. The Galadhrim guarded their borders carefully, and few outsiders were permitted to enter without their consent.

This isolation contributed to Lórien's strange reputation among neighboring peoples. The Rohirrim called the forest Dwimordene and regarded Galadriel with considerable suspicion, while many Men knew little about the actual nature of the realm. Its secrecy was not merely a matter of mystery, however; Lórien had survived for centuries in a region increasingly dominated by Orcs, hostile strongholds, and the returning power of Sauron.

The Fellowship Comes to Lórien

In January 3019, the Fellowship of the Ring entered Lórien after escaping from Moria. Gandalf had fallen while fighting Durin's Bane at the Bridge of Khazad-dûm, leaving the remaining members exhausted and uncertain of what they should do next. Elves led by Haldir encountered them near the borders and eventually guided them deeper into the realm.

Gimli's presence initially caused difficulty because Dwarves were not ordinarily admitted, particularly after the long estrangement that followed the destruction of Eregion and the later dangers associated with Moria. Aragorn insisted that the Company remain together, and Gimli was eventually permitted to proceed. The Fellowship traveled to Caras Galadhon, where they were received by Galadriel and Celeborn.

Their stay allowed the Company to recover from its passage through Moria and the loss of Gandalf. It also gave Galadriel an opportunity to learn more about the quest, and she understood that Frodo carried the One Ring. The Ring's arrival in Lórien confronted her personally with a choice that had consequences for both herself and her realm.

Galadriel Refuses the Ring

During the Fellowship's stay, Galadriel showed Frodo and Sam her Mirror, a basin in which they saw visions that could concern the past, present, or possible future. Afterward, Frodo offered her the One Ring, believing that someone of her wisdom and power might be able to use it against Sauron.

Galadriel understood both the temptation and the danger. The Ring could have increased her power enormously, but any attempt to use it would ultimately have corrupted her and replaced Sauron's domination with another form of tyranny. She therefore refused it, accepting that the destruction of the Ring would also mean the fading of Nenya and the eventual end of Lórien as she had preserved it.

When the Fellowship departed, Galadriel and Celeborn provided boats, food, Elven-cloaks, and other supplies for the journey down the Anduin. Galadriel also gave individual gifts, including the Phial of Galadriel to Frodo, a box of earth and a mallorn seed to Sam, and three strands of her hair to Gimli. Several of these gifts later became important: the phial aided Frodo and Sam against Shelob, while Sam used Galadriel's earth and mallorn seed during the restoration of the Shire.

The War Reaches the Golden Wood

The Fellowship left Lórien in February 3019, and within weeks the wider War of the Ring reached the realm directly. Sauron's northern forces operated from Dol Guldur while his main armies attacked Gondor, allowing him to strike simultaneously at several of the Free Peoples. Lórien, the Woodland Realm, Dale, and Erebor all came under attack during this period.

Forces from Dol Guldur assaulted Lórien three times, on 11 March, 15 March, and 22 March 3019. Each attack was repelled by the Galadhrim. Tolkien states that the realm might have suffered serious harm had it not been for the strength of its defenders and the power that dwelt there, indicating that Galadriel and the protection associated with Lórien remained formidable even during Sauron's final offensive.

The third attack occurred only days before the destruction of the One Ring. On 25 March 3019, Frodo and Sam reached Mount Doom, where the Ring was destroyed and Sauron's power collapsed. The armies dependent upon him were thrown into confusion, ending the immediate threat to Lórien and allowing the Elves to turn against Dol Guldur itself.

The Destruction of Dol Guldur

On 28 March 3019, Celeborn crossed the Anduin with an army of Lórien and captured Dol Guldur. Galadriel then threw down its walls and laid bare its pits, destroying the fortress from which Sauron and his servants had threatened the surrounding lands for centuries. The forest was cleansed, bringing the long struggle between Lórien and the power in southern Mirkwood to an end.

After the victory, Celeborn and Thranduil met in the forest and divided responsibility for its restored lands. Thranduil's realm extended southward, while the southern portion was associated for a time with Celeborn and was called East Lórien. The central region between them was given to the Beornings and Woodmen, reflecting the new political order that followed Sauron's defeat.

For Lórien, the destruction of Dol Guldur represented the greatest military success of its later history. Yet the same victory that removed its principal enemy also marked the end of the power that had helped preserve the Golden Wood for centuries.

The Fading of the Three Rings

When the One Ring was destroyed, the power of the Three Rings of the Elves faded with it. Nenya could therefore no longer sustain Lórien in the manner it had during the Third Age. Sauron's defeat saved the realm from conquest, but it also ensured that the preservation maintained by Galadriel could not continue indefinitely.

This was part of a wider change throughout Middle-earth. Elrond's Vilya and Gandalf's Narya likewise lost their power, while increasing numbers of the High Elves prepared to leave Middle-earth. The Fourth Age would belong principally to Men, and the surviving Elven realms could no longer remain unchanged remnants of earlier ages.

Lórien did not immediately become deserted. Galadriel and Celeborn remained there during the first years after the war, and the Galadhrim continued to inhabit the Golden Wood. Nevertheless, the historical conditions that had allowed Lothlórien to flourish as one of the great Elven refuges were coming to an end.

Galadriel's Departure

In Third Age 3021, Galadriel left Lórien and traveled west. She joined Elrond and others on the journey to the Grey Havens, where she sailed into the West with Elrond, Gandalf, Frodo, and Bilbo. Her departure ended the period in which Lórien had been one of the principal centers of Elven power in Middle-earth.

Celeborn remained in the Golden Wood after Galadriel's departure, but eventually he too left Lórien and went to Rivendell, where he lived for a time with Elrond's sons Elladan and Elrohir. Tolkien does not provide a precise date for Celeborn's final departure from Middle-earth, nor does he describe a sudden abandonment of Lórien. Instead, the realm appears to have gradually diminished as its Elven inhabitants departed or dwindled.

The Golden Wood therefore passed into the Fourth Age without being destroyed by an enemy. Its decline resulted from the departure of its people and the fading of the power that had preserved it, rather than from military conquest.

Arwen's Final Journey

The final major event Tolkien records in Lothlórien occurred more than a century after the War of the Ring. Arwen, daughter of Elrond and wife of King Elessar, had chosen mortality when she bound her fate to Aragorn. After Aragorn voluntarily surrendered his life in Fourth Age 120, Arwen left Minas Tirith and traveled north.

She returned to Lórien, but the realm she had once known had largely passed away. Galadriel had sailed into the West, Celeborn had left, and the Golden Wood was described as silent. Arwen went to Cerin Amroth, the hill where she and Aragorn had pledged themselves to one another during the Third Age.

There, in Fourth Age 121, Arwen died and was buried. Her death at Cerin Amroth forms a final connection between Lórien's long Elven history and the royal house of the Reunited Kingdom. Afterward, Tolkien records little more of the Golden Wood.

The Golden Wood

The history of Lothlórien extended from the early Silvan communities of the Anduin valleys into the Fourth Age. It existed before Galadriel and Celeborn became its rulers, survived Sauron's wars in the Second Age, lost King Amdír during the Last Alliance, and continued for centuries under his son Amroth. The fall of neighboring Khazad-dûm then transformed the region, leading indirectly to the departure of Nimrodel and the death of Amroth.

Under Galadriel and Celeborn, Lórien became one of the principal Elven refuges of the late Third Age. Nenya helped Galadriel preserve the realm, while the mallorn forests and Caras Galadhon gave it the form encountered by the Fellowship. At the same time, its position between Moria and Dol Guldur placed it close to two of the greatest dangers in the central lands of Middle-earth.

Lórien survived those dangers and defeated three attacks during the War of the Ring. After Sauron's fall, its forces captured Dol Guldur and Galadriel destroyed the fortress, ending a threat that had overshadowed the region for centuries. Yet the destruction of the One Ring also caused Nenya to lose its power, making the fading of Lórien inevitable even in victory.

The realm's end was therefore very different from the destruction of Eregion or the fall of Khazad-dûm. Lothlórien was not conquered, burned, or abandoned after a final battle. Its rulers and inhabitants gradually departed as the Third Age ended and the dominion of Men began, leaving the Golden Wood as one of the fading remnants of the Elven world that had preceded it.
