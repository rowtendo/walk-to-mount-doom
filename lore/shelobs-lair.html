The Darkness Above Minas Morgul

High in the Ephel Dúath, the Mountains of Shadow that formed Mordor's western wall, there was a passage through which few living things willingly traveled.

It lay above the dreadful valley of Minas Morgul, along the hidden road toward the pass of Cirith Ungol.

The tunnel was known as Torech Ungol.

Shelob's Lair.

Within it dwelt a creature older than Sauron's dominion in Mordor, a being that served neither Orc nor Ringwraith and acknowledged no master.

Her name was Shelob.

For long years she lived in darkness beneath the mountains, feeding upon whatever wandered into her webs.

And even the servants of Sauron feared her.

The Last Child of Ungoliant

Shelob was no ordinary spider.

She was the last known child of Ungoliant to trouble the unhappy world.

Ungoliant had been a monstrous being of darkness in the First Age, taking the form of an enormous spider. Her origins remained mysterious even to the Eldar.

For a time she allied herself with Morgoth, the first Dark Lord.

Together they entered Valinor.

There Morgoth struck the Two Trees, and Ungoliant drank their sap and poisoned them. She consumed the light stored within them and cast a vast darkness over the land.

Afterward she fled to Middle-earth.

In the lands south of Beleriand she bred with other creatures of spider form, producing horrors that infested dark valleys and mountains.

Among her descendants was Shelob.

Long after Ungoliant disappeared from the histories of the world, her brood remained.

And Shelob eventually came to the borders of Mordor.

A Hunger Older Than Barad-dûr

Shelob inhabited the mountains above what would become Minas Morgul before Sauron returned to Mordor.

She did not worship him.

She did not serve him.

Shelob cared little for Rings, kingdoms, wars, or the designs of Dark Lords.

She desired only to feed.

Sauron nevertheless found her useful.

He allowed her to remain in the pass, regarding her as a convenient guardian against anyone attempting to enter Mordor by that road.

From time to time, his servants became her prey.

Sauron tolerated even this.

Tolkien describes him with grim irony as thinking of Shelob as his cat, while Orcs served as the unfortunate creatures upon which she might feed.

Shelob herself had no concern for such arrangements.

She was independent of Sauron.

Their interests merely happened to overlap.

Cirith Ungol

Shelob's tunnels formed part of a dangerous route through the western mountains of Mordor.

From the Morgul Vale, a traveler climbed the Straight Stair and then the Winding Stair, ascending high above the valley.

Beyond lay Torech Ungol.

On the far side of the tunnels stood the Tower of Cirith Ungol.

The tower had originally been built by Gondor, not Mordor.

After Sauron's defeat at the end of the Second Age, Gondor constructed and maintained fortifications around Mordor to prevent evil things from returning to the Black Land.

As Gondor declined, the watch was abandoned.

Sauron eventually occupied the tower himself.

Thus, by the end of the Third Age, a traveler attempting the pass faced enemies on every side.

Behind lay Minas Morgul.

Ahead stood the Orc-tower.

Between them waited Shelob.

Gollum

One creature learned to pass through Shelob's domain and survive.

Gollum.

During his wanderings around Mordor, Gollum discovered the secret way through Cirith Ungol and encountered Shelob.

He feared her.

He also came to worship her in his own miserable fashion, bringing her whatever prey he could find.

In his mind, a plan gradually formed.

If he could bring travelers into her tunnels, Shelob might kill them.

Then, when she had finished feeding, Gollum could search what remained.

Years later, two Hobbits gave him the opportunity he had been waiting for.

One of them carried the One Ring.

The Secret Way

In March 3019, Frodo Baggins and Samwise Gamgee reached the Black Gate with Gollum as their guide.

The Morannon was impossible to enter unnoticed.

Gollum therefore told them of another road into Mordor.

He did not tell them what lived there.

He led the Hobbits south through Ithilien, past the Cross-roads, and toward the Morgul Vale.

They watched an army leave Minas Morgul.

Then they began the long ascent above the valley.

Higher and higher they climbed until they reached the entrance to a black tunnel.

Gollum disappeared.

Frodo and Sam entered.

Torech Ungol

The darkness inside Shelob's Lair was unlike ordinary night.

The passages twisted beneath the mountain.

The air became foul.

The Hobbits could feel webs and obstructions around them, while the stench of the tunnels grew increasingly oppressive.

Then Frodo remembered the gift he had received in Lothlórien.

The Phial of Galadriel.

He raised it.

Light shone in the darkness.

The phial contained light associated with the star of Eärendil, itself bearing the radiance preserved from a Silmaril.

Shelob recoiled.

She had descended from a creature that had once devoured light.

Now ancient light had entered her darkness.

Frodo and Sam forced their way through the webs and found the tunnel's exit.

For a moment, escape seemed possible.

Shelob had another way out.

The Betrayal

Gollum's plan now became clear.

Shelob emerged from another opening and came after Frodo.

Sam tried to warn him.

But at that moment Gollum attacked Sam from behind.

While the two struggled, Frodo continued ahead alone.

Shelob struck.

She pierced Frodo with her sting.

He collapsed.

By the time Sam reached him, Shelob was preparing to carry Frodo away.

Sam believed his master was dead.

Then he saw the creature that had taken him.

And grief became fury.

Samwise and Shelob

Sam was a gardener from the Shire.

He was small, exhausted, frightened, and deep within enemy country.

Shelob was an ancient monster who had fed upon Elves, Men, Orcs, and countless other creatures through long years of darkness.

Sam attacked her.

He carried Sting, the Elven blade that had belonged to Bilbo and then Frodo.

He also possessed the Phial of Galadriel.

Shelob attempted to crush him beneath her body.

Sam held Sting upright.

By the force of Shelob's own weight, the blade drove deeply into her.

Sam then raised the phial.

Its light blazed.

Shelob recoiled before it.

Wounded more severely than any opponent was known to have wounded her, she fled back into the darkness of her tunnels.

Sam did not pursue.

His concern was Frodo.

The Ring Comes to Sam

Sam returned to Frodo and found no sign of life.

Believing Frodo dead, he faced an unbearable decision.

The quest could not simply end beside the pass.

The Ring still had to be destroyed.

At last Sam took the One Ring from Frodo and prepared to continue toward Mount Doom alone.

For a brief time, Samwise Gamgee became the Ring-bearer.

Then Orcs arrived.

Sam used the Ring to hide and overheard their conversation.

From them he learned the truth.

Frodo was not dead.

Shelob's sting had paralyzed him.

The Orcs carried Frodo away to the Tower of Cirith Ungol.

Sam followed.

The quest had changed again.

What Became of Shelob?

Tolkien never gives a definite account of Shelob's final fate.

After Sam wounded her, she retreated deep into her lair.

Whether she eventually recovered from her injuries or slowly died in the darkness is left unknown.

That uncertainty is deliberate.

The narrative leaves Shelob where she had always been most terrible:

beyond sight, somewhere in the dark.

Her role in the War of the Ring was nevertheless finished.

She had failed to kill Frodo.

She had failed to take the Ring.

And the Hobbit she had nearly destroyed would soon awaken inside the Tower of Cirith Ungol.

Light in the Spider's Pass

Shelob's Lair was one of the darkest places upon Frodo's journey.

Yet its history is bound strangely to light.

In the First Age, Shelob's ancestor Ungoliant helped destroy the Two Trees and consumed their radiance.

A portion of the surviving light of those Trees was preserved in the Silmarils.

One Silmaril eventually came to Eärendil, who bore it into the heavens as a star.

Galadriel captured the light of that star in the water of her fountain and gave it to Frodo.

And in the tunnels above Mordor, Sam raised that light against Ungoliant's descendant.

A light whose history reached back to the Blessed Realm shone in a place that had known little but hunger and darkness.

Shelob fled before it.

The road remained terrible.

Frodo was taken.

Sam was left alone.

Yet the Ring continued toward Mount Doom.

And in a war filled with armies, kings, fortresses, and ancient powers, one of its most desperate victories belonged to a Hobbit gardener standing beneath the Mountains of Shadow with an Elven blade in one hand and a small glass of light in the other.
