The Pass Above Minas Morgul

Shelob's Lair, known as Torech Ungol, lay high in the western mountains of Mordor near the pass of Cirith Ungol. The tunnels pierced the Ephel Dúath, the Mountains of Shadow, above the valley of Minas Morgul and formed part of a hidden route into Mordor from Ithilien.

The pass did not provide an easy entrance into Sauron's realm. Travelers approaching from the west first had to climb the long stairways above the Morgul Vale before entering a network of dark passages occupied by Shelob. Beyond the tunnels lay the upper pass and the road toward the Tower of Cirith Ungol.

By the end of the Third Age, Torech Ungol had become one of the most dangerous ways into Mordor. Its danger came not primarily from Sauron's fortifications but from the ancient creature that had made the tunnels her home long before Frodo and Sam entered them.

Ungoliant and Shelob's Ancestry

Shelob belonged to a lineage extending back to Ungoliant, the monstrous being who aided Morgoth in the destruction of the Two Trees of Valinor during the First Age. Ungoliant later fled into Middle-earth and bred with other creatures of spider-form in the dark regions of the world.

Tolkien describes Shelob as the greatest surviving descendant of Ungoliant in Middle-earth and as the last child of Ungoliant to trouble the unhappy world. Her ancestry connected the tunnels above Cirith Ungol with evils far older than Sauron's final kingdom in Mordor.

The precise details of Shelob's birth and early movements are not recorded. Tolkien does not provide a complete chronology for her before she settled in the Mountains of Shadow, so her ancient history must be reconstructed cautiously from the few references he gives.

Shelob Comes to the Mountains of Shadow

Shelob had established herself in the mountains above what later became Minas Morgul long before the War of the Ring. She occupied deep tunnels beneath the pass and survived by hunting whatever creatures entered her domain.

Her lair was not described as a fortress constructed by Sauron or as part of his organized defenses. Instead, Shelob lived independently in the tunnels and treated the region as her own hunting ground.

This distinction was important because Shelob did not serve Sauron in the way that Orcs, Nazgûl, or other subjects did. She followed her own hunger and interests, while Sauron tolerated her presence because she made the pass dangerous to intruders.

Shelob and Sauron

Sauron knew that Shelob lived above the Morgul Vale and regarded her as a useful obstacle to anyone attempting to enter Mordor by that route. Tolkien describes their relationship in terms suggesting that Sauron amused himself by feeding prisoners to her and valued the danger she created.

Shelob, however, was not under his direct command. She did not obey orders or act as part of Mordor's military hierarchy, and her motives were entirely her own.

The arrangement benefited Sauron without requiring him to control her. Anyone attempting the hidden route toward Cirith Ungol had to survive both Shelob's tunnels and the defenses beyond them, making the pass dangerous even before the traveler reached a formal Mordorian stronghold.

Cirith Ungol and Gondor

The pass itself had once been watched by Gondor. After Sauron's defeat at the end of the Second Age, Gondor established fortifications around Mordor to prevent his servants from returning.

The Tower of Cirith Ungol, standing on the eastern side of the pass, was originally one of these Gondorian defenses. Its purpose was to watch the route through the Mountains of Shadow rather than to keep enemies out of Mordor.

As Gondor weakened during the Third Age, the watch on Mordor declined and many of these positions were abandoned. The Tower of Cirith Ungol eventually fell into the hands of Sauron's servants and became part of the very defensive system it had originally been built to oppose.

Shelob's tunnels therefore lay between two different kinds of danger: her independent lair and the later Orc-held fortress beyond it.

Gollum Discovers the Pass

At some point before the War of the Ring, Gollum discovered the route through Cirith Ungol and became familiar with Shelob's tunnels. He survived encounters with her and learned enough about the pass to use it when traveling in and out of Mordor.

Gollum developed a peculiar relationship with Shelob. Tolkien indicates that he worshipped her in his own fashion and sometimes brought victims into her reach.

His knowledge of Torech Ungol later became central to his plan involving Frodo and Sam. When the Black Gate proved impossible to enter secretly, Gollum offered Cirith Ungol as an alternative without revealing the danger waiting in the tunnels.

The Black Gate Is Closed

In March 3019, Frodo Baggins and Samwise Gamgee reached the Morannon, the Black Gate of Mordor, under Gollum's guidance. Their intention had been to enter through the northern gate because they knew of no other route.

Once they saw the defenses, it became clear that two Hobbits could not pass through unnoticed. Sauron's armies were moving through the gate, and the approaches were closely watched.

Gollum then urged them to turn south toward another entrance. He described a hidden way above Minas Morgul and claimed that it offered a better chance of entering Mordor.

Frodo accepted the proposal because no practical alternative was available. Gollum did not tell him that the route led directly through Shelob's lair.

Gollum's Betrayal

Gollum's plan depended upon using Shelob to remove Frodo and Sam. He hoped that she would kill the Hobbits, after which he could recover the One Ring from whatever remained.

His motives combined his desire for the Ring with his growing resentment of Sam and his unstable attachment to Frodo. For a time, Frodo's kindness had encouraged the better side of Gollum's divided personality, but that possibility gradually collapsed during the journey.

By the time the travelers approached Cirith Ungol, Gollum had committed himself to betrayal. He led Frodo and Sam toward the tunnels while concealing both Shelob's presence and his expectation that she would attack them.

The Morgul Vale

Before reaching Torech Ungol, the travelers entered the valley beneath Minas Morgul. The city had once been Gondor's Minas Ithil but had been captured by the Nazgûl and transformed into one of Sauron's principal strongholds.

From hiding, Frodo, Sam, and Gollum witnessed an army leaving Minas Morgul under the command of the Witch-king. The force marched toward Gondor as part of Sauron's assault that would culminate in the Battle of the Pelennor Fields.

After the army passed, the travelers began climbing the stairs above the valley. The route rose steeply through the mountains and eventually brought them to the entrance of the tunnels.

This placed Torech Ungol directly beside one of the principal military routes associated with Mordor's western defenses, even though Shelob herself remained independent of those armies.

The Tunnels of Torech Ungol

Inside the tunnels, Frodo and Sam entered almost complete darkness. The passages were filled with a powerful stench and with webs strong enough to obstruct their progress.

Gollum disappeared during this stage of the journey, leaving the Hobbits to move forward without his guidance. His absence was part of his betrayal, because Shelob could attack while he avoided becoming one of her victims.

The darkness of the tunnels also weakened ordinary means of navigation. Frodo and Sam could not easily determine where the passages led, and the enclosed space made retreat difficult once they had penetrated deeply into the lair.

Their most important protection came from a gift Frodo had received in Lothlórien.

The Phial of Galadriel

Frodo carried the Phial of Galadriel, which contained light associated with the star of Eärendil. Galadriel had given it to him as a source of light in dark places.

Within Torech Ungol, the Phial became more than an ordinary lamp. Its light resisted the supernatural darkness surrounding Shelob and gave Frodo and Sam enough illumination to find their way through the tunnels.

The Phial also had a powerful effect upon Shelob. She recoiled from its light, which carried a brightness connected through Eärendil's Silmaril to the ancient light of the Two Trees.

This confrontation linked the events in Shelob's tunnel to much older history. A descendant of Ungoliant, whose darkness had helped destroy the Trees, was opposed by light ultimately derived from one of their surviving Silmarils.

Shelob Attacks

Frodo and Sam eventually escaped the main tunnels and moved toward the upper pass. Their immediate danger seemed to have diminished, but Shelob had followed them through another route.

At the same time, Gollum attacked Sam and tried to prevent him from helping Frodo. Sam fought him off, but the struggle separated the two Hobbits long enough for Shelob to strike.

Shelob attacked Frodo from behind and stung him in the neck. Her venom paralyzed him, leaving him apparently lifeless.

Sam returned too late to prevent the attack and found Frodo lying motionless beneath Shelob.

Sam Fights Shelob

Sam first drove Gollum away and then turned against Shelob. Though vastly smaller than the creature, he attacked her using Sting, the Elven blade that Frodo had carried since receiving it from Bilbo.

Shelob attempted to crush Sam beneath her body, but this movement helped drive Sting deeply into her underside. The wound was more severe than any she had previously suffered from an enemy.

Sam also used the Phial of Galadriel, invoking its light against her. The brightness became intolerable to Shelob and contributed to her retreat.

The combination of the wound and the Phial forced Shelob away from the fight. Sam therefore succeeded in doing what few others had apparently managed: he survived direct combat with her and inflicted a serious injury.

Shelob's Fate

Tolkien does not state with certainty whether Shelob died from her wounds. After retreating into her tunnels, she disappeared from the narrative.

The text suggests that she may have spent a long time attempting to heal herself, but her eventual fate is deliberately left unresolved. There is no later account confirming either her recovery or her death.

Any claim that Shelob was certainly killed by Sam therefore goes beyond what Tolkien records. Sam wounded and drove her away, but the final outcome for Shelob remains unknown.

Sam Believes Frodo Is Dead

After Shelob fled, Sam returned to Frodo and tried unsuccessfully to wake him. Unable to detect signs of life, he believed that Frodo had died.

Sam then faced a difficult decision about the quest. If Frodo were dead, the One Ring still had to be taken to Mount Doom, and Sam concluded that he would have to continue the task himself.

He took the Ring from Frodo and briefly became a Ring-bearer. He also kept the Phial and Sting as he prepared to continue toward Mordor.

This decision was made out of duty rather than ambition. Sam did not intend to keep or use the Ring for his own purposes; he believed he was taking responsibility for Frodo's unfinished mission.

The Orcs Arrive

Before Sam could leave, Orcs from the nearby Tower of Cirith Ungol discovered Frodo's body. Sam used the Ring to hide and followed closely enough to overhear their conversation.

The Orcs recognized the signs of Shelob's attack and understood something Sam had not: Frodo was not dead. Shelob's sting had paralyzed him so that she could later feed upon him.

The discovery changed Sam's immediate priority. Frodo was alive but had been taken by the Orcs into the Tower of Cirith Ungol.

Sam followed them toward the fortress, still carrying the Ring. Shelob's attack had therefore separated the Hobbits temporarily, but it had not ended Frodo's journey.

The Tower of Cirith Ungol

The Orcs carried Frodo into the Tower of Cirith Ungol, where rival groups from the tower and Minas Morgul argued over the prisoner and his possessions. Their dispute eventually turned violent.

Sam entered the fortress after much of the Orc garrison had destroyed itself through internal fighting. He found Frodo alive and returned the One Ring to him.

The two Hobbits then disguised themselves using Orc equipment and escaped into Mordor. Their passage through Torech Ungol had nearly ended the quest, but it ultimately placed them inside Sauron's realm with the Ring still in their possession.

From there, they crossed the plateau of Gorgoroth toward Mount Doom.

Torech Ungol and the Quest of the Ring

Shelob's Lair became one of the most dangerous stages of Frodo and Sam's journey because it combined Gollum's betrayal with a threat older than Sauron's current rule. The Hobbits were not simply trying to evade Orc patrols or pass through a fortified border; they had entered the hunting ground of a creature descended from the darkness of the First Age.

The encounter also changed Sam's role in the quest. He fought Shelob, took possession of the Ring when he believed Frodo dead, discovered the truth from the Orcs, and then entered the Tower of Cirith Ungol to rescue his master.

Frodo's temporary capture had significant consequences as well. His possessions eventually reached Sauron's servants and were later displayed by the Mouth of Sauron at the Black Gate, though Sauron still failed to understand that Frodo had carried the Ring into Mordor for the purpose of destroying it.

Torech Ungol therefore became a point where the quest came extremely close to failure without revealing its true purpose to Sauron.

The Spider's Pass

The history of Shelob's Lair is less fully documented than that of major kingdoms or fortresses. Tolkien gives no detailed account of its construction, no complete chronology of Shelob's settlement there, and no definitive description of what happened to her after Sam wounded her.

What is known places the tunnels within a much broader history. Shelob descended from Ungoliant, settled independently in the Mountains of Shadow, and remained there while Gondor's watch over Mordor rose and fell and Sauron later reclaimed the surrounding land.

By the War of the Ring, her presence had become indirectly useful to Sauron because the pass offered intruders a danger beyond his formal defenses. Gollum exploited that same danger when he deliberately brought Frodo and Sam into her reach.

The encounter in Torech Ungol ultimately failed to stop the Ring-bearers. Sam drove Shelob away, discovered that Frodo was still alive, and helped him escape from Cirith Ungol into Mordor. What had been intended as Gollum's final betrayal instead became one of the last obstacles the Hobbits survived before crossing Gorgoroth and reaching Mount Doom.
