The Founding of Imladris

Rivendell, called Imladris in Sindarin, was an Elven refuge hidden in a deep valley near the western foothills of the Misty Mountains. The river Bruinen, or Loudwater, flowed through the surrounding country, while steep slopes and difficult approaches helped conceal the settlement from hostile travelers. By the end of the Third Age, Rivendell had become one of the principal centers of Elven learning and memory in Middle-earth.

Its origins, however, lay not in peace but in war. During the Second Age, the Noldorin realm of Eregion flourished west of Khazad-dûm under the leadership of Celebrimbor and the great Elven-smiths known as the Gwaith-i-Mírdain. Sauron, appearing in a fair form as Annatar, deceived the smiths and helped guide their work before secretly forging the One Ring in Mount Doom.

When the Elves became aware of Sauron's betrayal, war followed. In Second Age 1695, Sauron invaded Eriador, and two years later Eregion was destroyed and Celebrimbor was killed. Elrond, who had been sent east by Gil-galad with a force from Lindon, gathered survivors and withdrew northward, establishing a refuge in the hidden valley that became Imladris.

The War of the Elves and Sauron

Rivendell was therefore founded in Second Age 1697 as a military and defensive refuge. Elrond's position helped block Sauron's expansion through northern Eriador, while forces from Khazad-dûm and Lórinand also resisted Sauron from the east. The settlement survived the war even though much of the surrounding country had been devastated.

The balance shifted when the Númenóreans intervened. King Tar-Minastir sent a great fleet under the command of Ciryatur, and Númenórean forces joined Gil-galad against Sauron. By Second Age 1701, Sauron's armies had been driven out of Eriador.

After the war, Rivendell remained in use rather than being abandoned. It became Gil-galad's principal eastern stronghold and one of the most important surviving Elven centers in the region. What had begun as a temporary refuge consequently developed into a permanent settlement under Elrond.

Elrond and Vilya

The history of Rivendell was inseparable from the history of its lord. Elrond Half-elven was born near the end of the First Age, the son of Eärendil and Elwing, and through them descended from both Elves and Men as well as from the great royal houses of the Elder Days.

After the War of Wrath, Elrond and his brother Elros were allowed to choose which kindred they would belong to. Elrond chose to be counted among the Elves, while Elros chose the fate of Men and became the first king of Númenor. This made Elrond a distant kinsman of the royal lines that later descended from Elros through the Númenórean kingdoms.

At some point after the fall of Eregion, Gil-galad entrusted Vilya to Elrond. Vilya, the Ring of Air, was the mightiest of the Three Rings of the Elves. Its power helped Elrond preserve and protect Rivendell through the long centuries of the Second and Third Ages, although the exact nature of that preservation is never described mechanically by Tolkien.

The Last Alliance

Near the end of the Second Age, Sauron again became the principal enemy of the Free Peoples. After the Downfall of Númenor, Elendil and his sons founded the kingdoms of Arnor and Gondor, and when Sauron attacked Gondor, Elendil joined with Gil-galad to form the Last Alliance of Elves and Men.

Rivendell became closely associated with the alliance. The great host gathered in the region before crossing the Misty Mountains and marching east, and Elrond accompanied Gil-galad as his herald. This placed Elrond at the center of the final campaign against Sauron at the end of the Second Age.

The alliance eventually entered Mordor and besieged Barad-dûr. Gil-galad and Elendil were killed in the final struggle, while Sauron was overthrown and Isildur cut the One Ring from his hand. Elrond witnessed Isildur's refusal to destroy the Ring, an event whose consequences would later return directly to Rivendell more than three thousand years afterward.

Rivendell and Arnor

During the Third Age, Rivendell became increasingly connected with the history of Arnor, the northern kingdom of the Dúnedain. After Arnor divided into Arthedain, Cardolan, and Rhudaur in Third Age 861, the northern Dúnedain gradually weakened and became involved in long wars against the kingdom of Angmar.

Rivendell served as both ally and refuge during these conflicts. Elrond aided the Dúnedain when Angmar attacked the northern kingdoms, and the valley remained one of the few strong places in Eriador that the Witch-king never conquered. Its hidden location and Elrond's power made it a secure refuge even as the surrounding political order collapsed.

When Arthedain finally fell in Third Age 1974, the royal kingdom of Arnor ceased to exist as a functioning state. The line of Isildur, however, survived, and Rivendell became one of the principal places where that line was preserved.

The Heirs of Isildur

After the death of Arvedui, the last king of Arthedain, his son Aranarth became the first Chieftain of the Dúnedain rather than claiming the title of king. The northern Dúnedain became a wandering people whose Rangers secretly guarded Eriador without openly ruling it.

The sons of the Chieftains were fostered in Rivendell, where they could grow up in safety and learn the history of their people. Important heirlooms of the House of Isildur were also kept there, including the shards of Narsil, the sword that had broken beneath Elendil during the final battle against Sauron.

For roughly a thousand years, Rivendell therefore served as a guardian of the political and cultural inheritance of Arnor. The kingdom itself had disappeared, but its royal line, its heirlooms, and its memory endured under Elrond's protection.

Aragorn

The last and most important of the heirs raised at Rivendell was Aragorn II. He was born in Third Age 2931, the son of Arathorn II and Gilraen, and was brought to Rivendell after his father was killed when Aragorn was only two years old.

Elrond accepted the child into his household and concealed his identity for his protection. Aragorn was called Estel, meaning "Hope," and he grew up without initially being told that he was the heir of Isildur and Chieftain of the Dúnedain.

In 2951, when Aragorn was twenty years old, Elrond revealed his ancestry and gave him the heirlooms of his house, including the shards of Narsil and the Ring of Barahir. That revelation marked the point at which Aragorn ceased to be merely a protected heir and began consciously taking up the responsibilities of his lineage.

Aragorn and Arwen

Shortly after learning his true identity, Aragorn met Arwen, Elrond's daughter, who had returned to Rivendell after spending many years with Galadriel and Celeborn in Lothlórien. Aragorn fell in love with her, beginning a relationship that would ultimately bind the history of Rivendell to the restoration of Arnor and Gondor.

Arwen was one of the Half-elven and therefore inherited the choice of kindred associated with Elrond's family. By choosing Aragorn, she would eventually accept the mortality of Men and separate herself from Elrond beyond the end of the world. Elrond understood the seriousness of that choice and would not agree to their marriage unless Aragorn fulfilled his inheritance.

After Aragorn and Arwen pledged themselves to one another in Lothlórien, Elrond made clear that Arwen would marry no lesser man than the king of both Gondor and Arnor. Rivendell therefore became not only the place where Aragorn's ancestry had been preserved but also the home from which his future kingship and marriage were measured.

Bilbo Baggins Comes to Rivendell

In Third Age 2941, Rivendell received visitors of a very different kind. Bilbo Baggins, Gandalf, and the thirteen Dwarves led by Thorin Oakenshield arrived while traveling east toward the Lonely Mountain.

Elrond examined the swords Glamdring and Orcrist, identifying them as ancient weapons forged in Gondolin during the First Age. He also interpreted the moon-letters on Thrór's Map, revealing the hidden instructions concerning the secret entrance into Erebor.

The company later passed through Rivendell again on the return journey. Bilbo retained a deep affection for the place, and after leaving the Shire many years later he eventually settled there, spending his old age writing, translating Elven lore, and working on his memoirs.

A House of Lore

By the late Third Age, Rivendell had become one of the principal repositories of knowledge in Middle-earth. Elrond's own memory reached back almost to the end of the First Age, and his household preserved histories, genealogies, songs, heirlooms, and traditions that had disappeared from most other regions.

The settlement was therefore important not only as a refuge but also as a place where different peoples and histories could meet. Elves, Dúnedain, Dwarves, Wizards, and other travelers could find counsel there, while the surviving knowledge of earlier ages helped shape decisions in the present.

Elrond's household included his sons Elladan and Elrohir, his daughter Arwen when she was in residence, and powerful Elves such as Glorfindel. Rivendell's importance depended less upon territorial rule than upon the people, memory, and knowledge preserved within the valley.

The White Council

Elrond was also a member of the White Council, formed to oppose the growing threat of the Necromancer at Dol Guldur. Other members included Galadriel, Gandalf, Saruman, and additional figures among the Wise.

The Council eventually determined that the Necromancer was Sauron returned. In Third Age 2941, it moved against Dol Guldur, forcing Sauron to withdraw, although he had already prepared for the move and soon returned openly to Mordor.

Rivendell was therefore closely connected to the wider resistance against Sauron long before the War of the Ring began. Its role was primarily one of counsel, preservation, and coordination rather than direct military expansion.

Frodo Reaches Rivendell

In Third Age 3018, Rivendell became central to the fate of the One Ring. After leaving the Shire, Frodo Baggins was wounded by the Witch-king at Weathertop with a Morgul-knife, and Aragorn led him east toward Rivendell while the wound steadily worsened.

Near the Ford of Bruinen, the travelers encountered Glorfindel, whom Elrond had sent to search for them. When the Nazgûl closed in, Frodo rode ahead across the river, and the Ringwraiths followed him into the ford.

The Bruinen then rose in flood and swept the Nazgûl away. Elrond commanded the river, while Gandalf later explained that he had added the appearance of white horses and riders to the flood. Frodo was carried into Rivendell, where Elrond removed the fragment of the Morgul-blade and healed him.

The Council of Elrond

On 25 October 3018, representatives of several Free Peoples gathered at Rivendell. Many had arrived independently for different reasons, but their presence allowed Elrond to bring together knowledge that had been scattered among Elves, Men, Dwarves, Hobbits, and Wizards.

At the Council of Elrond, the history of the One Ring was reconstructed. Gandalf described Sauron's return and Saruman's betrayal, Aragorn's ancestry and Isildur's history were discussed, Boromir spoke for Gondor, and Glóin brought news concerning the Dwarves and Sauron's attempts to obtain information about Bilbo.

The Council considered whether the Ring could be hidden, used, or sent elsewhere, but each alternative was rejected. The conclusion was that the Ring had to be destroyed in the place where it had been made: Mount Doom. Frodo volunteered to carry it.

The Fellowship of the Ring

The Company that would accompany Frodo was chosen at Rivendell. It consisted of Frodo, Sam, Merry, Pippin, Aragorn, Boromir, Legolas, Gimli, and Gandalf, bringing together representatives of the principal Free Peoples involved in the struggle against Sauron.

Before the Fellowship departed, the shards of Narsil were reforged into a new sword. Aragorn named it Andúril, the Flame of the West, restoring the weapon of Elendil at the moment when Elendil's heir was preparing to enter the final war against Sauron.

The Fellowship left Rivendell on 25 December 3018. The departure connected several strands of Rivendell's history at once: the heir of Isildur carried the reforged sword of Elendil, Frodo carried the Ring that Isildur had failed to destroy, and Elrond sent them from the same refuge that had preserved both memories for thousands of years.

Rivendell During the War of the Ring

Rivendell itself was not the site of a major battle during the War of the Ring. Its contribution came through the people, knowledge, and heirlooms it had preserved and through the decisions made there before the Fellowship departed.

Elrond's sons Elladan and Elrohir later rode south with the Grey Company and joined Aragorn. They accompanied him through the Paths of the Dead and participated in the campaign that brought him to Gondor before the Battle of the Pelennor Fields.

Elrond also sent counsel through his sons, reminding Aragorn to consider the Paths of the Dead. This advice helped shape Aragorn's decision to summon the Dead Men of Dunharrow and use their aid against the Corsairs threatening southern Gondor.

The Restoration of the King

On 25 March 3019, the One Ring was destroyed and Sauron's power collapsed. Aragorn was later crowned King Elessar, restoring the kingship of Gondor and ultimately the northern realm of Arnor as part of the Reunited Kingdom.

For Rivendell, this fulfilled a history stretching back more than a thousand years. The heirs of Isildur had been fostered there after the fall of Arthedain, their heirlooms had been preserved there, and the last heir had grown up there under Elrond's protection.

The restoration of the northern kingdom therefore vindicated Rivendell's role as guardian of Arnor's inheritance. What had survived only as memory, lineage, and heirlooms during the long years of the Rangers once again became a functioning royal realm.

The Marriage of Aragorn and Arwen

After Aragorn's coronation, Elrond traveled south to Gondor. On Midsummer's Day in 3019, Aragorn and Arwen were married in Minas Tirith.

Their marriage reunited two branches of the Half-elven family that had been separated since the beginning of the Second Age. Elrond and Elros had been brothers, but Elros chose mortality and became the first king of Númenor, while Elrond chose the Elven fate. Aragorn descended from Elros, while Arwen was Elrond's daughter.

The marriage fulfilled Elrond's condition that Aragorn achieve his inheritance before marrying Arwen. It also meant that Arwen accepted mortality and would eventually be separated from Elrond when he departed Middle-earth.

The Fading of Vilya

The destruction of the One Ring brought a major change to Rivendell. The power of the Three Rings of the Elves faded, and Vilya could no longer preserve Elrond's realm in the same way it had during the Third Age.

Sauron's defeat therefore saved Rivendell from the threat of conquest but also ended the conditions that had allowed it to remain one of the great preserved Elven refuges of Middle-earth. The same change affected Lothlórien through Nenya and marked the broader fading of the Elves.

Rivendell did not disappear immediately, but its great age was ending. The beginning of the Fourth Age belonged increasingly to Men, and many of the High Elves prepared to leave Middle-earth.

Elrond's Departure

In Third Age 3021, Elrond left Rivendell and traveled west to the Grey Havens. He sailed from Middle-earth with Galadriel, Gandalf, Frodo, and Bilbo, ending a lordship of more than four thousand seven hundred years.

Elrond's sons Elladan and Elrohir remained in Rivendell for a time after his departure. Tolkien does not provide a precise account of their ultimate fate, and Celeborn also came to dwell in Rivendell for a period after leaving Lothlórien.

The valley therefore did not become empty as soon as Elrond sailed. Nevertheless, the departure of its lord and the fading of Vilya marked the end of Rivendell's role as one of the principal centers of Elven power in Middle-earth.

The Last Homely House

Rivendell endured from Second Age 1697 into the beginning of the Fourth Age, surviving longer than many kingdoms and cities that had once surrounded it. It began as a refuge for survivors of Sauron's destruction of Eregion and developed into a permanent stronghold, a center of learning, and a sanctuary for the heirs of the northern Dúnedain.

Its history was shaped by preservation. Rivendell preserved Elven knowledge from the Elder Days, sheltered the line of Isildur after the fall of Arnor, guarded the shards of Narsil, gave Bilbo a home in his old age, healed Frodo after Weathertop, and brought together the knowledge necessary for the Council of Elrond to determine the fate of the One Ring.

Few places demonstrate the continuity of Tolkien's history as clearly. Elrond had witnessed Isildur refuse to destroy the Ring at the beginning of the Third Age, and more than three thousand years later he presided over the council that decided the Ring must be taken back to Mount Doom. The same house that had preserved Isildur's heirs also sent Aragorn toward the kingship that restored Arnor.

Rivendell's importance therefore lay less in conquest than in endurance. It preserved people, memories, objects, and knowledge long enough for them to become important again. When Elrond finally departed, the Ring had been destroyed, Aragorn had become king, and the historical tasks that Rivendell had quietly sustained for centuries had largely been completed.
