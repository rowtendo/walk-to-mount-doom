Amon Sûl

Weathertop, called Amon Sûl in Sindarin, was the southernmost and highest of the Weather Hills in central Eriador. It stood north of the Great East Road, roughly midway between Bree and Rivendell, and its height gave it commanding views across the surrounding country.

During the early Third Age, Amon Sûl was an important stronghold of Arnor, the northern kingdom founded by Elendil after the Downfall of Númenor.

A great watchtower stood upon its summit. Its importance was not merely military: the tower housed one of the palantíri, the Seeing-stones brought to Middle-earth by Elendil and his sons.

The presence of this Stone eventually made Amon Sûl one of the most contested places in the history of the North-kingdom.

The Tower of Amon Sûl

The tower was built during the early history of Arnor, though Tolkien does not provide a precise date for its construction.

Within it was kept the Stone of Amon Sûl, one of the seven palantíri distributed among the realms of Arnor and Gondor.

Three of the seven were kept in the North: at Annúminas, Amon Sûl, and Elostirion in the Tower Hills.

The Amon Sûl Stone was particularly important. It was the principal palantír of the northern realm and the largest of the three northern Stones.

Through the palantíri, their lawful users could communicate across great distances and perceive faraway places. Amon Sûl therefore served both as a physical watchtower and as an important center of communication for the Dúnedain.

For centuries it overlooked a united kingdom.

That changed in Third Age 861.

The Division of Arnor

After the death of King Eärendur, Arnor was divided among his three sons.

The northern kingdom became three smaller realms: Arthedain in the northwest, Cardolan in the south, and Rhudaur in the northeast.

Amon Sûl stood close to the borders of all three.

This immediately created a problem.

The three kingdoms disputed possession of the Weather Hills, largely because each wanted control of the palantír kept there.

Arthedain possessed the Stone of Annúminas and claimed the inheritance of the senior line of Isildur. Cardolan and Rhudaur disputed that claim.

The tower that had once watched over a united Arnor therefore became a strategic prize in the rivalries of its successor states.

Those rivalries weakened the Dúnedain at precisely the time when a new enemy was beginning to gather in the north.

The Rise of Angmar

Around Third Age 1300, the realm of Angmar arose beyond the northeastern borders of the divided kingdoms.

Its ruler was the Witch-king, though the Dúnedain did not initially know that he was the chief of the Nazgûl.

Angmar's purpose was to destroy the surviving realms of Arnor.

Rhudaur was the first to fall under its influence. The native Dúnedain there dwindled, and power passed to Men allied with Angmar.

Cardolan and Arthedain continued to resist.

This made the Weather Hills an increasingly important frontier.

Amon Sûl, standing between the competing northern realms and commanding the surrounding lands, became one of the principal defenses against Angmar's expansion.

The Attack of 1409

In Third Age 1409, the Witch-king launched a major invasion of the Dúnedain kingdoms.

Angmar's forces crossed the river Hoarwell, entered the lands around the Weather Hills, and attacked Amon Sûl.

The tower was destroyed.

The Dúnedain defending it were defeated, and King Arveleg I of Arthedain was slain.

The palantír, however, was saved.

The survivors carried the Stone of Amon Sûl westward to Fornost, the capital of Arthedain.

Cardolan was devastated during the same war. Although remnants of its people continued to resist, particularly in the region of the Barrow-downs and the Old Forest, it never fully recovered as an independent kingdom.

Amon Sûl was not rebuilt.

From this point onward, the summit of Weathertop was crowned not by a functioning fortress but by the ruins of the old watchtower.

The Lost Seeing-stone

Although the tower was gone, its palantír survived for more than five centuries.

The Stone remained in Arthedain and was eventually held at Fornost.

Then, in Third Age 1974, Angmar launched its final assault upon the North-kingdom.

Fornost fell.

King Arvedui, the last king of Arthedain, fled northward and eventually took refuge among the Lossoth of Forochel.

He carried with him the palantíri of Amon Sûl and Annúminas.

In 1975, Arvedui attempted to escape aboard a ship sent from Lindon by Círdan. The vessel was caught in a storm and sank in the Bay of Forochel.

Arvedui died.

Both Seeing-stones were lost beneath the sea.

Thus the palantír that had made Amon Sûl so important in the wars of the northern kingdoms disappeared with the last king of Arthedain.

After the Fall of the North-kingdom

Angmar itself was defeated later in Third Age 1975, when forces from Gondor, Lindon, Rivendell, and the surviving Dúnedain destroyed the Witch-king's army at the Battle of Fornost.

But the kingdom of Arnor was not restored.

The surviving Dúnedain became a wandering people led by the Chieftains of the Dúnedain. Their Rangers guarded the remaining settlements of Eriador, including Bree and the Shire, while the ruins of the old northern kingdom slowly faded into the landscape.

Weathertop remained one of those ruins.

By the end of the Third Age, travelers could still see the broken remains of the ancient tower upon its summit, but the place no longer held a permanent garrison.

The Great East Road passed to its south, and the hill remained a useful landmark for anyone traveling between Bree and Rivendell.

Its strategic value had not disappeared simply because its tower had fallen.

Gandalf at Weathertop

Weathertop returned briefly to the center of history in Third Age 3018, during the opening stages of the War of the Ring.

The Nazgûl had entered Eriador searching for Frodo Baggins, who was carrying the One Ring toward Rivendell.

Gandalf, attempting to find Frodo before the Ringwraiths did, traveled east from Bree.

He reached Weathertop on 3 October.

That night he was attacked by several of the Nazgûl.

From a distance, Aragorn and the Hobbits later saw evidence of the struggle as flashes of light upon the hill. Gandalf fought the Ringwraiths through the night and escaped northward at dawn, deliberately drawing some of them after him.

Before leaving, he marked a stone to indicate that he had been there.

When Aragorn and the Hobbits reached Weathertop several days later, Aragorn discovered the mark.

They were not alone for long.

The Attack on Frodo

On 6 October 3018, Aragorn led Frodo, Sam, Merry, and Pippin to Weathertop.

That night, five Nazgûl approached their camp.

The Hobbits gathered around a fire while Aragorn prepared to defend them.

Frodo was overwhelmed by the desire to put on the One Ring.

When he did so, the Ringwraiths became far more clearly visible to him. He saw them as pale figures wearing ancient garments beneath their black outer robes.

Their leader was the Witch-king of Angmar.

The same enemy who had destroyed the kingdom of Arthedain and whose armies had ruined the tower of Amon Sûl centuries earlier had now returned to its remains.

The Witch-king stabbed Frodo in the shoulder with a Morgul-knife.

Frodo invoked the name of Elbereth and struck at his attacker, while Aragorn drove the Ringwraiths away with fire.

The wound, however, was extremely dangerous.

A fragment of the Morgul-blade remained inside Frodo and began working toward his heart. Had it reached him, Frodo would have faded and become a wraith under the domination of the Nazgûl.

Aragorn therefore abandoned the direct road and hurried the Hobbits toward Rivendell.

From Weathertop to Rivendell

The journey from Amon Sûl became a race against Frodo's wound.

Aragorn treated him with athelas, which eased his suffering but could not remove the fragment.

The company traveled through the wild country toward the Ford of Bruinen.

Near Rivendell they encountered Glorfindel, who placed Frodo upon his horse and sent him ahead when the Nazgûl attacked.

Frodo crossed the Bruinen.

The Ringwraiths followed and were swept away when the river rose against them, through the power of Elrond, with the flood given additional form by Gandalf.

In Rivendell, Elrond removed the fragment of the Morgul-blade.

Frodo survived.

But the wound never entirely ceased to trouble him. On its anniversary he continued to suffer pain, one of several lasting injuries that eventually contributed to his decision to leave Middle-earth.

A Monument to the North-kingdom

Weathertop is important because its history connects two very different periods of the Third Age.

Long before the War of the Ring, Amon Sûl had been a center of royal power in Arnor. Its palantír made it valuable enough for the successor kingdoms to dispute, and its destruction marked one of the great defeats suffered by the Dúnedain during the wars with Angmar.

By Frodo's lifetime, almost none of that political world remained.

Annúminas was deserted.

Fornost was ruined.

The palantír of Amon Sûl lay beneath the northern sea.

The descendants of Arnor's people survived mainly as Rangers, and ordinary inhabitants of Eriador knew little of their history.

The ruined tower upon Weathertop was therefore more than scenery along the road to Rivendell.

It was evidence of how much had been lost.

There is also a direct historical connection between the two most important events associated with the hill.

In 1409, the forces of the Witch-king destroyed Amon Sûl and killed King Arveleg.

More than sixteen centuries later, in 3018, the Witch-king returned to the ruins and wounded Frodo there.

The first event belonged to his long campaign to destroy the heirs of Isildur in the North.

The second occurred while one of those heirs—Aragorn—was secretly guiding the Ring-bearer toward Rivendell.

Amon Sûl had once been a watchtower of the kingdom Aragorn was destined to restore.

By the time he stood upon it, only the stones remained.

The following year, after the defeat of Sauron, Aragorn became King Elessar of the Reunited Kingdom of Arnor and Gondor.

The tower of Amon Sûl was not restored in Tolkien's recorded history.

But the kingdom to which it had once belonged finally was.
