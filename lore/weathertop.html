Amon Sûl

Weathertop, called Amon Sûl in Sindarin, was the southernmost and highest of the Weather Hills in central Eriador. It stood north of the Great East Road, roughly midway between Bree and Rivendell, and its height gave it commanding views across the surrounding country. During the early Third Age, Amon Sûl was an important stronghold of Arnor, the northern kingdom founded by Elendil after the Downfall of Númenor.

A great watchtower stood upon its summit. Its importance was not merely military: the tower housed one of the palantíri, the Seeing-stones brought to Middle-earth by Elendil and his sons. The presence of this Stone eventually made Amon Sûl one of the most contested places in the history of the North-kingdom.

The Tower of Amon Sûl

The tower was built during the early history of Arnor, though Tolkien does not provide a precise date for its construction. Within it was kept the Stone of Amon Sûl, one of the seven palantíri distributed among the realms of Arnor and Gondor.

Three of the seven Stones were kept in the North: at Annúminas, Amon Sûl, and Elostirion in the Tower Hills. The Amon Sûl Stone was the chief palantír of the northern realm and the largest of the three northern Stones. Through the palantíri, their lawful users could communicate across great distances and perceive faraway places, making Amon Sûl both a physical watchtower and an important center of communication for the Dúnedain.

For centuries the tower served the united kingdom of Arnor. That changed after the death of King Eärendur in Third Age 861.

The Division of Arnor

After Eärendur's death, disagreements among his three sons led to the division of Arnor into three smaller kingdoms: Arthedain in the northwest, Cardolan in the south, and Rhudaur in the northeast. Amon Sûl stood near the borders of all three, and possession of the tower soon became a matter of dispute.

The palantír was one of the principal reasons for this rivalry. Arthedain possessed the Stone of Annúminas and was ruled by the senior line descended from Isildur, while Cardolan and Rhudaur also sought control of Amon Sûl and its Seeing-stone. The fortress that had once served a united Arnor consequently became a strategic prize among its successor kingdoms.

These divisions weakened the Dúnedain of the North. In the centuries that followed, that weakness would be exploited by a new enemy.

The Rise of Angmar

Around Third Age 1300, the kingdom of Angmar arose beyond the northeastern borders of the Dúnedain realms. Its ruler was the Witch-king, although the Dúnedain did not initially know that he was the chief of the Nazgûl. His purpose was to destroy the surviving kingdoms of Arnor.

Rhudaur was the first of the three kingdoms to fall under Angmar's influence. The Dúnedain there dwindled, and control eventually passed to Men allied with the Witch-king. Arthedain and Cardolan continued to resist, making the Weather Hills an important frontier between the remaining Dúnedain and their enemies.

Amon Sûl therefore became significant for more than its palantír. Its commanding position and existing fortifications made it one of the principal defensive sites in the wars between the northern Dúnedain and Angmar.

The Fall of Amon Sûl

In Third Age 1409, the Witch-king launched a major invasion. The forces of Angmar crossed the Hoarwell, entered the lands around the Weather Hills, and attacked Amon Sûl. King Arveleg I of Arthedain was killed, and the great tower upon Weathertop was burned and destroyed.

The defenders nevertheless succeeded in saving the palantír. The Stone of Amon Sûl was carried westward to Fornost, where it remained under the protection of Arthedain. Cardolan was devastated during the same invasion. Although some of its people continued to resist, particularly in the region of the Barrow-downs and the Old Forest, the kingdom never recovered its former strength.

The tower of Amon Sûl was never rebuilt. From 1409 onward, the summit of Weathertop was crowned by the ruins that remained visible in Frodo's time more than sixteen centuries later.

The Lost Seeing-stone

The palantír of Amon Sûl survived the destruction of its tower for more than five hundred years. It remained in Arthedain until Third Age 1974, when Angmar launched its final campaign against the North-kingdom. Fornost fell, and King Arvedui, the last king of Arthedain, fled northward before the Witch-king's armies.

Arvedui eventually took refuge among the Lossoth near the Bay of Forochel. He carried with him the palantíri formerly kept at Amon Sûl and Annúminas. In 1975, Círdan sent a ship from Lindon to rescue him, but after Arvedui boarded, the vessel was caught in a storm and wrecked in the icy waters of the bay. Arvedui and the two Seeing-stones were lost.

The palantír that had contributed to centuries of conflict over Amon Sûl therefore disappeared with the last king of Arthedain. Of the three northern Stones, only the palantír kept at Elostirion remained.

The End of the North-kingdom

Although Arthedain had fallen, Angmar's victory was brief. Later in Third Age 1975, an army from Gondor arrived in the North under Prince Eärnur, joining the Elves of Lindon and the surviving Dúnedain. At the Battle of Fornost, the forces of Angmar were defeated and its kingdom was destroyed.

The Witch-king escaped, however. When Eärnur attempted to pursue him, his horse recoiled in terror. Glorfindel of Rivendell then made his famous prediction that the Witch-king would not fall by the hand of man. More than a thousand years later, that prophecy would be fulfilled during the Battle of the Pelennor Fields.

Despite Angmar's destruction, Arnor was not immediately restored. The surviving Dúnedain became a wandering people led by the Chieftains of the Dúnedain, beginning with Arvedui's son Aranarth. Their Rangers secretly protected much of Eriador, while the cities and fortresses of the old kingdom fell increasingly into ruin.

Weathertop became one such relic. Its ruined tower remained a landmark above the Great East Road, but there was no longer a permanent settlement or garrison upon the hill.

Weathertop in the Late Third Age

By the time of the War of the Ring, the political world that had created Amon Sûl had largely disappeared. Annúminas was deserted, Fornost lay in ruins, and the palantír of Amon Sûl had been lost beneath the northern sea. The descendants of Arnor's people survived chiefly as the Rangers of the North, while most inhabitants of Eriador knew little of their ancestry or of the kingdom they had once defended.

The Great East Road still passed south of Weathertop, however, and the hill remained a useful landmark for travelers moving between Bree and Rivendell. Its height also retained the same strategic advantage that had originally made it valuable to Arnor: anyone occupying the summit could observe a wide area of the surrounding country.

That importance returned suddenly in Third Age 3018, when the Nazgûl entered Eriador in search of the One Ring.

Gandalf at Weathertop

After escaping from Saruman and reaching Eriador, Gandalf attempted to find Frodo before the Nazgûl could intercept him. He passed through Bree and continued east, reaching Weathertop on 3 October 3018.

That night, Gandalf was attacked on the hill by several of the Ringwraiths. He resisted them with fire and light throughout the night and escaped at dawn, drawing some of the Nazgûl away toward the north. Aragorn and the Hobbits, still approaching Weathertop from the west, saw flashes in the distance that were later understood to have been signs of Gandalf's battle.

Before leaving, Gandalf scratched a mark upon a stone to show that he had reached the hill. Aragorn found evidence of his passage when the Hobbits arrived several days later.

The Attack on Frodo

On 6 October 3018, Aragorn reached Weathertop with Frodo, Sam, Merry, and Pippin. The company was being pursued by the Nazgûl, and that night five Ringwraiths approached their camp.

During the attack, Frodo was overcome by the urge to put on the One Ring. Wearing it allowed him to perceive the Ringwraiths more clearly than he could in the ordinary world. Among them he saw their leader, the Witch-king of Angmar.

The Witch-king stabbed Frodo in the shoulder with a Morgul-knife. Frodo invoked the name of Elbereth and struck at his attacker, while Aragorn drove the Nazgûl away with fire. The wound was nevertheless extremely dangerous: a fragment of the Morgul-blade remained inside Frodo and began working inward toward his heart. If it had reached him, Frodo would eventually have faded and become a wraith under the domination of the Nazgûl.

There was a remarkable historical continuity in this encounter. The Witch-king who attacked Frodo was the same enemy whose armies had destroyed the tower of Amon Sûl in 1409 and later brought about the fall of Arthedain. More than sixteen centuries after the tower's destruction, the chief enemy of the old North-kingdom had returned to its ruins.

The Flight to Rivendell

Aragorn treated Frodo with athelas, which helped ease the wound but could not remove the fragment of the Morgul-blade. The company therefore left Weathertop and continued east toward Rivendell, traveling through the wilderness rather than remaining upon the exposed road.

They eventually encountered Glorfindel, who had been sent from Rivendell to search for them. As the Nazgûl closed in near the Ford of Bruinen, Glorfindel placed Frodo upon his horse, Asfaloth, and sent him ahead toward the river. Frodo crossed the ford, and when the Ringwraiths followed, the Bruinen rose in flood. The flood was commanded by Elrond, with Gandalf later explaining that he had added the appearance of great white horses to its waters.

Frodo was taken to Rivendell, where Elrond removed the fragment of the Morgul-blade. He recovered, but the wound never completely ceased to trouble him. Its lasting effects were among the injuries that prevented Frodo from finding complete peace after the War of the Ring.

A Monument to the North-kingdom

Weathertop is important because its history connects the rise and fall of Arnor with the events of the War of the Ring. It began as a fortified watch-hill of the northern Dúnedain, housing one of their most important palantíri. After Arnor divided, control of the hill became a source of conflict among its successor kingdoms, and Amon Sûl eventually stood on the front line of the long wars against Angmar.

Its destruction in 1409 was part of the gradual collapse of the northern Dúnedain kingdoms. The loss of its palantír in 1975 and the extinction of Arthedain as a functioning kingdom left the ruined tower as a reminder of a political order that had disappeared from Eriador.

This history gives Frodo's arrival at Weathertop additional significance. He was attacked there by the same Witch-king who had spent centuries destroying the northern Dúnedain, while his protector, Aragorn, was the direct heir of the royal line that had survived those wars. Aragorn himself had been raised in Rivendell as the heir of Isildur and was serving as Chieftain of the Dúnedain when he led the Hobbits through the ruins.

In Third Age 3019, less than a year after the attack on Weathertop, Sauron was defeated and Aragorn became King Elessar. Through him the northern and southern lines of the Dúnedain were reunited, and the kingdom of Arnor was restored as part of the Reunited Kingdom.

Tolkien does not record the reconstruction of the tower of Amon Sûl. Its ultimate fate in the Fourth Age is therefore unknown. What is known is that the kingdom whose frontiers it had once guarded finally existed again.
